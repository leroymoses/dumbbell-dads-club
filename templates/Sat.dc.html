Myth Busting2/6
The origin

It was a sales pitch

The number traces back to a 1960s Japanese pedometer called the "manpo-kei." It means "10,000-step meter." It was marketing, not medicine.

Dad take: A catchy number is not a prescription.

The research

Benefits level off

Where the curve flattensUnder 60: about 8,000 to 10,000 a day
60 and up: about 6,000 to 8,000 a day

Paluch et al., 2022, The Lancet Public Health. Pooled data from 15 studies.

Translation: More helps, up to a point.

The real goal

Beat your own number

If you average 4,000 now, 10,000 is a fantasy. Add 1,000 a day this week. Then another 1,000 next week.

Dad tip: Check your phone's weekly average. That's your starting line.
